blurb: |
  <p>Glenn Dale opened in 1934 as one of the largest tuberculosis
  sanatoriums in the country, built by the District of Columbia to treat
  DC-area TB patients on a sprawling campus of Georgian Revival brick
  buildings. After 1960, as tuberculosis declined, the hospital shifted to
  treating District residents with other chronic illnesses. It closed for
  good in 1982. The county acquired the property in 1995, and the site was
  added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2011 — but it has
  sat empty the whole time, still waiting on a redevelopment plan that
  hasn't materialized.</p>
