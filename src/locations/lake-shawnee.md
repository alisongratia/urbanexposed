blurb: |
  <p>Lake Shawnee opened in 1926, built by Conley Trigg Snidow Sr. for the
  coal-mining families of the area — a Ferris wheel, a swing ride, a
  swimming pool, a race track, a dance hall, and cabins for overnight
  stays. Two drownings and the death of a young girl on the swing ride in
  1966 were followed, that same year, by the park failing a health
  inspection and closing.</p>
  <p>A former employee bought the land in 1985 and briefly reopened it in
  1987. In 1988, an archaeological dig on the property turned up Native
  American artifacts and human remains predating European settlement, and
  the park closed for good that year — officially over the cost of
  insurance. The rides and structures are still standing.</p>
